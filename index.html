Abstract

As robots are increasingly deployed in human-centered environments, ranging from collaborative manufacturing to security surveillance, the ability to perceive and interpret motions of other robots is becoming essential. In particular, accurate perception of robotic hand poses and motions is critical for multi-robot interaction and coordination. While human hand pose estimation has reached a relatively mature stage, vision-based 3D pose estimation for robotic hands remains largely underexplored, limiting both the understanding of robot behavior and cross-platform skill transfer among heterogeneous robotic systems. We identify three key challenges underlying this problem: (1) the structural diversity of robotic end effectors; (2) the scarcity of annotated 3D pose data for robotic hands; and (3) severe occlusions and weak visual textures inherent to mechanical structures. To address these challenges, we propose $\textbf{ManiTrack}$, a unified RGB-D estimation pipeline for real-time 3D robotic hand pose estimation. ManiTrack incorporates hand-specific kinematic priors to recover kinematically consistent hand states from unreliable visual observations. Extensive experiments show that the online variant of ManiTrack achieves an average AUC@50 of $\textbf{79.89\%}$ across three dexterous hands on a synthetic dataset at $\textbf{19.73 FPS}$, while generalizing to real-world observations without fine-tuning. Finally, we demonstrate its practical utility through a robot motion imitation task.
